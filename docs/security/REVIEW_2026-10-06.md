# Security review, 2026-10-06: money movements

**Scope.** The code that moves money or reverses it, changed during the deep browser test's fixes. That is credit notes, refunds and the use of a customer's or supplier's credit (#280), payments, voiding an invoice, and the reversal of journal entries. Read line by line, then each finding reproduced in a test before it was fixed.

**Result.** Six problems, all fixed in #289. Each could leave the books wrong or pay money out twice; none needed more than ordinary books-write access. Amounts are validated as positive whole numbers, and every endpoint here is tenant-scoped and permission-checked. Those were sound.

| # | Problem | What could happen | Fix |
|---|---|---|---|
| S1 | The ledger's reverse route (`POST /manager-reports/journal/{id}/reverse`) had no "already reversed" guard and left no mark | The same entry reversed again on every call; a void afterwards reversed it once more | `LedgerService.reverse_journal_entry` refuses a second reversal (409) and writes the "undo" audit row every reversal path checks |
| S2 | Voiding an invoice reversed its recognition and payments, not its credit notes | A voided invoice left sales returns, VAT and a customer credit on the books, and that credit could still be refunded: money out for an invoice that no longer exists | A void reverses the credit notes too, and is refused while the invoice's credit has been refunded or used and not reversed |
| S3 | A reversed refund, use or overpayment still counted in the app | Reversing a "paid by credit" payment restored the credit in the books but not in the app; a reversed overpayment's credit stayed refundable | The credit left counts live entries only: a reversed refund or use gives its amount back, and a credit whose own entry was reversed is gone |
| S4 | No row locks around spending credit | Two refunds at once (a double-click, a retry) could both pay out the same credit; two credit notes at once could both pass the "left to credit" check | Payments, credit notes, refunds, uses of credit and voids lock the invoice row; a refund or a use of credit then locks the credit rows it draws on, always after the invoice |
| S5 | A refund's or payment's bank account could be any account, and an unknown code fell back to the bank silently | A refund posted against capital or an expense account; a mistyped code paid from the default bank | Only a bank, cash, petty-cash or cheque account is accepted (422 otherwise): a received cheque pays an invoice into cheques receivable |
| S6 | A cheque that paid more than its invoice, then bounced or was handed back, posted the whole amount back onto the receivable | Its extra stayed as customer credit and could be refunded for money never received; the receivable came out too high; depositing the cheque again made a second credit | The bounce or return takes the extra back out of the credit (or the supplier advance), marks it withdrawn, and is refused while it was refunded or used |

**Tests.** `tests/test_credit_integrity.py`, scenarios J1–J6 in `docs/qa/SCENARIOS.md`. The ten SQLite tests fail without the fixes, the four cheque ones included. The lock test runs on PostgreSQL: a second refund waits for the first, then finds nothing left (409); it uses a 5 s lock timeout so a regression fails rather than hangs.

**Not changed, noted.** Reversals are marked by "undo" audit rows rather than a column on the transaction; every path now writes one, which the tests check. A column would be cleaner if this mark is ever needed in SQL joins at scale.
